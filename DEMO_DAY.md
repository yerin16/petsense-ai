# Demo Day story · slide notes

The professor's Maker Day 3 feedback: the wedge was clear, and the pivot based on data and the sequencing of dependent experiments (in Q&A) landed well. The gaps were: it wasn't clear the top 5 came from a register or a rigorous ranking, why the top 3 are existential, or what the sequencing logic was. Fix these on the Demo Day slides (Jessie and Charlotte own them):

1. **Show the register.** One slide: all 29 hypotheses scored doubt × importance, with the top 5 highlighted.
2. **Explain the tie-breaker.** The top 5 are all tied at 9, which is why the ranking looked arbitrary. Rank them with one stated rule, e.g. "if this is false, does the whole product fail?" and "do other hypotheses depend on it?"
3. **Say why each top risk is existential:** "If this is false, then ___."
   - Voluntary opening: if owners don't open PetSense first, nothing else matters.
   - Logging in ≤ 2 min: if logging is slow, PetSense has no history to use.
   - Trust in AI advice: if owners don't trust the guidance, they keep waiting and guessing.
4. **Explain the sequence:** open it → give it data → trust and act on it. Each test only matters if the one before it passes, and the build order follows the same chain.

**Every top-5 hypothesis has a plan:**

| Top-5 hypothesis | How we address it |
|---|---|
| Voluntary opening | Experiment 1 retest (adoption), build step 4 |
| Logging in ≤ 2 min | Experiment 2 (logging), build step 5 |
| Trust in AI advice | Experiment 3 (trust), build step 6 |
| Safe red-flag escalation | Deliberately not tested before Demo Day: a future safety gate that needs a clinician-reviewed test set. Hard-coded red-flag rules are built in step 7, but we make no safety claim |
| Urgency over wellness | Covered by the "Should I wait?" pitch and the Home screen leading with new symptoms; the adoption retest uses new-symptom scenarios |
